Six completely fresh candidate poems about permission — the power to ask and to be given.
Written for v2 after v1 ("The Stone in My Pocket") scored 72.07% and ranked #492.
None of these reuses v1's images (stone, door, key, gate, a child asking) or the
current top entries' images (a ladder, a seat, salt, the controls, borrowed hands).
Each centres on the YES / the gift / the being-given as the climax. The chosen one is marked CHOSEN.

--- A. Bread (the warm half of a loaf) ---
I asked for bread.
She broke the loaf in two
and gave me the warm half.
I held it in both hands,
still steaming from the oven —
the whole of the yes,
warm against my palms.
Note: the warm half is vivid and the ending is sensory, but "the whole of the yes" is a slightly conceptual phrase, and the bread's warmth is a less distinctive image than a lamp left burning all night.

--- B. The lamp left on (a bed for the night) — CHOSEN ---
I asked for a bed.
She made up the spare room,
smoothed the clean sheets,
and left the lamp on.
All night the light
stayed in the doorway —
her yes, still burning.
Note: chosen. The most tender and specific image (a lamp left on in a spare room), the ask is plain, the gift is shelter, and the ending — "her yes, still burning" — makes the yes the climax and lets it persist all night.

--- C. The umbrella (the dry circle) ---
It was raining.
I asked if I could share
her umbrella. She moved over,
and the rain stopped
on my shoulders. Under it,
two people, one dry circle,
and the rain, politely,
falling everywhere else.
Note: the dry circle is a lovely image and the ending is imagistic, but the poem never names the yes, so the permission theme is implied rather than felt.

--- D. The match (a light for the candle) ---
The candle had gone out.
I asked for a light.
He struck a match — one flare,
the smell of sulfur,
and the wick caught.
The dark stepped back
from the table. That is a yes:
a flame, handed over.
Note: the flare and the sulfur are vivid, but the poem is a little mechanical (strike, catch, dark steps back) and less emotionally resonant than the lamp or the bread.

--- E. The flashlight (the beam, the way home) ---
The path was dark.
I asked for the flashlight.
She pressed it into my hand —
one click, and the beam
found the stones, the turn,
the way home. The dark
had nowhere left to stand.
Note: the beam finding the way home is a strong image and the ending is good, but "the way home" is a slightly familiar phrase and the poem is about a tool more than a gift.

--- F. The boots (two sizes too big) ---
I was barefoot.
I asked for shoes.
She gave me her boots,
two sizes too big,
and I walked in them all winter.
By spring, they fit —
as if my feet had grown
into her yes.
Note: growing into her yes is a strong turn, but the poem spans a whole season, so it is less a single moment of being given than the lamp or the bread.
